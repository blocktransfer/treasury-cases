## 6. Research point — Fifth Amendment limits on compelled tax-record production

**Primary authority:** [*United States v. Porter*, 711 F.2d 1397, 1404–06 (7th Cir. 1983)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/421677/united-states-of-america-and-gerald-t-padar-special-agent-internal/) (reversing in part and remanding an order enforcing IRS summonses). See also the [district court decision, 557 F. Supp. 703 (N.D. Ill. 1983)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2239527/united-states-v-porter/).

**Facts and holding:** The IRS summoned records from a sole proprietor, Beligratis, and his attorney, Porter, including canceled checks, bank deposit slips, and business books. Other records had already been produced; the Seventh Circuit treated objections to their production as moot. For the remaining checks and deposit slips, it held that the *act of producing* the documents could have a protected testimonial aspect under the Fifth Amendment, because production could implicitly authenticate them. The court reversed the unconditional production order and remanded to allow statutory use immunity for the testimonial act of production plus a protective order, while leaving open use of the documents' contents if independently authenticated. *Id.* at 1398–1400, 1402–06; see [*Fisher v. United States*, 425 U.S. 391 (1976)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/109432/fisher-v-united-states/).

**Specific treatment of 26 USC § 6001:** The government invoked the *required-records doctrine*, arguing that the statutory recordkeeping obligation under [26 USC § 6001](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6001/) and [26 CFR § 1.6001-1(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.6001-1) defeated the Fifth Amendment objection. The Seventh Circuit rejected that contention **for these private checks and deposit slips**. Unlike records maintained as a condition of participation in a comprehensive regulatory scheme, the sole proprietor's financial documents did not acquire sufficient public characteristics merely because tax law required records for tax administration. *Porter*, 711 F.2d at 1404–05 (discussing [*Shapiro v. United States*, 335 U.S. 1 (1948)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/104585/shapiro-v-united-states/) and [*Marchetti v. United States*, 390 U.S. 39 (1968)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/107606/marchetti-v-united-states/)).

**Limits and relevance to this case:** *Porter* **did not invalidate § 6001**, excuse taxpayers from keeping records otherwise required by law, or decide whether receipts or postmark evidence must be retained. It concerns the Fifth Amendment limits on **compelled production** in the context of an IRS investigation with potential criminal implications, not the ordinary evidentiary burden of proving timely mailing under [26 USC § 7502](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s7502/). Furthermore, the dispute here concerns an **S corporation**, rather than a sole proprietor asserting an individual privilege; the collective-entity rule in [*Braswell v. United States*, 487 U.S. 99 (1988)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112120/braswell-v-united-states/) materially limits a corporate custodian's ability to resist producing corporate records on personal Fifth Amendment grounds.

**Follow-up research:** Assess whether any specific IRS demand in the present correspondence actually seeks compelled production, as distinguished from requesting evidence supporting a penalty-abatement claim; identify its precise statutory or regulatory basis before invoking *Porter*. Treat the case as useful authority on the distinction between **record retention** and **compelled disclosure**, not as a direct solution to the missing-postmark dispute.

## 7. Research point — Shapiro and the required-records doctrine

**Primary authority:** [*Shapiro v. United States*, 335 U.S. 1, 17–20, 32–35 (1948)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/104585/shapiro-v-united-states/) (majority opinion of Chief Justice Vinson). *Shapiro* became a principal Supreme Court foundation for the **required-records doctrine**: documents that a person must maintain under a valid regulatory scheme may have sufficiently public characteristics that their compelled production does not implicate the ordinary Fifth Amendment privilege over private papers. The doctrine was **not created from nothing in 1948**: the Court relied on [*Wilson v. United States*, 221 U.S. 361 (1911)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1293085/wilson-v-united-states/), and [*Davis v. United States*, 328 U.S. 582 (1946)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/104313/davis-v-united-states/). *Shapiro*, 335 U.S. at 17–20, 32–35.

**Facts and decision:** Shapiro operated a licensed wholesale produce business subject to the wartime Emergency Price Control Act. Government investigators subpoenaed sales invoices, sales books, ledgers, inventories, and related transaction records that regulations required him to maintain. He asserted the privilege against self-incrimination and claimed immunity following their production. The Supreme Court affirmed his conviction, holding that these lawfully required business records possessed **public aspects** and that their compelled disclosure did not entitle him to the claimed statutory immunity. The records related directly to transactions in a business the government could regulate and license. *Id.* at 3–6, 17–20, 32–35.

**What “public aspects” meant in *Shapiro*:** The records were sales records that Shapiro's licensed wholesale produce business had to maintain under wartime price-control regulations. The regulatory scheme made those records available for government inspection as part of the business's regulated operations; it did **not** mean the records were published or accessible to the general public. Their “public aspects” came from their prescribed regulatory function and the government's lawful inspection authority, not from public visibility. See [*Shapiro*, 335 U.S. at 32–35](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/104585/shapiro-v-united-states/).

**Why it matters doctrinally:** *Shapiro* is the leading decision extending the required-records rationale to records kept by an **individual operating an unincorporated business**, not just corporate books. The majority also acknowledged limits to governmental power to demand records and did **not** hold that *every* paper becomes a public record simply because Congress requires someone to keep it. *Id.* at 17–19, 32–35. Justice Frankfurter dissented, warning against the erosion of constitutional protection for private papers when the government mandates recordkeeping; this is a **dissent**, not the holding.

**Later refinements and contrast with tax records:** The Supreme Court later articulated the doctrine's familiar criteria in [*Grosso v. United States*, 390 U.S. 62, 67–68 (1968)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/107607/grosso-v-united-states/): an essentially regulatory purpose, records customarily maintained by the regulated party, and records with public aspects. See also [*Marchetti v. United States*, 390 U.S. 39, 56–57 (1968)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/107606/marchetti-v-united-states/). In [*United States v. Porter*, 711 F.2d 1397, 1404–05 (7th Cir. 1983)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/421677/united-states-of-america-and-gerald-t-padar-special-agent-internal/), the Seventh Circuit expressly distinguished *Shapiro* and rejected the assertion that [26 USC § 6001](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6001/) and [26 CFR § 1.6001-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.6001-1), by themselves, converted a sole proprietor's canceled checks and deposit slips into required public records outside the Fifth Amendment privilege.

**Application to the postmark dispute:** These authorities concern the constitutional consequences of **compelling production of existing regulatory records**. They do not establish a legal duty to create or preserve a postmark photograph, receipt, or copy of a mailed return's envelope. Nor does *Shapiro* decide whether such evidence is sufficient to establish timely filing under [26 USC § 7502](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s7502/). Research these questions separately; distinguish the individual-privilege holdings from the corporation's procedural and evidentiary position.

**Research status:** Foundational precedent for understanding *Porter* and the required-records exception, not an independent ground for abating the S corporation's late-filing penalty.
