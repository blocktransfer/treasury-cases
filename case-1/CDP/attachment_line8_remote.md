## I. Introduction and Scope of Request

- Request a Collection Due Process hearing concerning the IRS's proposed levy.
- Identify each tax period and liability being challenged.
- Dispute the underlying liability, subject to the limitations of 26 USC 6330(c)(2)(B).
- Request review of the legality and appropriateness of the proposed collection action.
- Identify the relief sought.

## II. Request for Correspondence-Only Proceedings

- Request that the hearing be conducted exclusively through written correspondence.
- Decline telephone and in-person conferences as the preferred methods of communication.
- Request that all communications, questions, document requests, proposed findings, and determinations be provided in writing.
- Request sufficient opportunity to respond to documents and arguments provided by the IRS.
- Request notice and an opportunity to respond before Appeals closes the administrative record or issues its determination.

## III. Verification of Statutory and Administrative Requirements

Under 26 USC 6330(c)(1), request verification that applicable legal and administrative requirements have been met.

### A. Assessment Procedures

- Identify each assessment underlying the proposed levy.
- Request the applicable account and assessment transcripts.
- Examine assessment dates, amounts, and statutory authority.
- Verify that required assessment procedures were followed.

### B. Notice Requirements

- Review the statutory authority for each required notice.
- Verify issuance and legally sufficient delivery of applicable notices.
- Examine the taxpayer's opportunities to dispute the assessments.
- Identify any procedural deficiencies affecting collection.

### C. Collection Authority

- Identify the statutory authority for the proposed levy.
- Determine whether any restrictions on collection apply.
- Examine the applicable collection limitations period and any suspension or extension.

## IV. Challenge to the Underlying Tax Liability

Subject to 26 USC 6330(c)(2)(B):

### A. Basis of Liability

- Identify the tax provisions under which liability is asserted.
- Dispute any incorrect factual or legal determinations.
- Present any applicable statutory interpretation arguments.

### B. Assessment Amounts

- Review the original amounts assessed.
- Examine adjustments, credits, and payments.
- Identify computational discrepancies.

### C. Penalties and Interest

- Identify each penalty and its statutory basis.
- Examine whether the requirements for imposing penalties were satisfied.
- Review interest calculations and applicable accrual periods.

### D. Eligibility to Challenge Liability

- Identify whether the taxpayer received a statutory notice of deficiency.
- Determine whether the taxpayer otherwise had a prior opportunity to dispute the liability.
- Explain why consideration of the underlying liability is permitted, if applicable.

## V. Appropriateness of the Proposed Levy

Under 26 USC 6330(c)(2)(A) and (c)(3):

- Challenge any procedural or legal defects in the proposed collection action.
- Examine whether the proposed levy is appropriate in light of the taxpayer's circumstances.
- Consider whether legitimate concerns about intrusiveness have been properly balanced against the need for efficient tax collection.
- Identify any applicable grounds for withdrawing, modifying, or suspending the proposed levy.

## VI. Requests for Records and Supporting Information

Request copies of or access to relevant materials, including:

- IRS account transcripts for each disputed period.
- Assessment records and supporting computations.
- Copies of relevant notices and available mailing records.
- Records of payments, credits, penalties, and interest.
- Records supporting the proposed collection action.
- Other documents materially relied upon by Appeals in its determination.

## VII. Preservation and Supplementation of Issues

- Identify each disputed assessment, legal issue, and procedural objection as specifically as practicable.
- Reserve the opportunity to supplement the written submission with additional evidence and legal arguments.
- Request an opportunity to respond to new information or explanations provided by the IRS.
- Request that Appeals consider all properly raised issues and address material objections in its determination.

## VIII. Requested Relief

The taxpayer requests that the IRS Independent Office of Appeals:

1. Conduct the CDP hearing through written correspondence.
2. Verify compliance with applicable statutory and administrative requirements.
3. Consider all properly raised challenges to the underlying liabilities.
4. Review the validity and appropriateness of the proposed levy.
5. Provide a reasonable opportunity to examine and respond to relevant supporting information.
6. Withdraw or refrain from sustaining the proposed levy to the extent warranted by the record and applicable law.
7. Issue a written Notice of Determination explaining the disposition of the material issues raised.
